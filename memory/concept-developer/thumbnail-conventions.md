# Thumbnail conventions

White line work on dark charcoal background. Hand-drawn feel.

## Conventions
- One central figure or object dominates the frame.
- One sharp text overlay — 2–4 words max — in white.
- The viewer's eye should land on the text overlay first, then the figure.
- Numbers are powerful: years, dollar amounts, percentages. Use sparingly.
- Iconic objects signal time period without explicit dates (Model T, top hat, flip phone).
- No faces of real people. If an institution matters, use its logo or a building silhouette.
- Avoid red — saves it for genuine emergencies. Default palette is white-on-dark; one accent color (warm yellow or cold blue) per video.

## 2026-05-15 — News in image, thesis in text

When an episode pairs a current event with an evergreen mechanism:
- The thumbnail **image** carries the news peg (a recognizable current-event element — a chokepoint, a building, a logo, a known location).
- The thumbnail **text overlay** carries the evergreen thesis.
- The two must complete each other, not repeat each other.
- The video title (below the thumbnail) delivers the contradiction in words; the overlay confirms the conclusion in 2–4 words.

Color logic (when using multiple marker colors):
- BLACK = structural / factual elements (geography, labels, neutral nouns)
- RED = the news / what's blocked / what just broke
- GREEN = money / the thesis / what survives / what wins

A scrolling viewer should be able to read the entire argument in under a second.
