# Episode template — the 5 beats

Every script follows this structure. Timestamps are at ~150 words/minute and are estimates, not constraints.

---

## Cardinal rule — fulfill the title-and-thumbnail promise inside 30 seconds

The title and thumbnail set up a specific curiosity gap. The viewer clicked because of it. The cold open MUST explicitly confirm — within the first 30 seconds — that the episode will deliver on that gap. Then the script may detour into history, context, or backstory, but only after the viewer knows the promise is binding.

The rule:
1. **Name the promise out loud, plainly.** Do not be coy. Do not bury the answer to "make them wait."
2. **Then over-deliver.** Add stakes the title didn't promise — a number, a count of historical repeats, a named beneficiary. The viewer should leave 0:30 thinking: *"they're going to tell me, and they're going to tell me bigger than I expected."*
3. **Then — and only then — pivot** to history, context, or the slower build.

Worked example for "Saudi Arabia sells dollars, not oil." with thumbnail "DOLLAR WILL SURVIVE":

> "This week the Strait of Hormuz shut. Oil hit $110. The biggest supply shock by volume in modern history. And by Friday, the dollar will be stronger than it was on Monday. This has happened seven times since 1974. Every time, the same handful of people got richer. Here's the trick."

That hook (1) confirms the promise (the dollar will survive), (2) raises stakes the thumbnail didn't promise (seven times, same beneficiaries), and (3) earns the next 12 minutes by promising to explain "the trick."

This rule overrides the generic hinge sentence in Beat 1 below when they conflict.

---

## Beat 1 — Cold open (0:00–0:30)

Apply the Cardinal rule above. The cold open's primary job is to **confirm the title-and-thumbnail promise** and **raise the stakes**. The generic hinge sentence — *"But while everyone argued about X, somebody made a billion dollars."* — is available but optional; use it only when it serves the specific promise the title and thumbnail set up.

---

## Beat 2 — The question (0:30–1:00)

Pivot from outrage to investigation. State the question that frames the whole episode:

> "So let's ask the only question that actually matters: who profits?"

Use a slight variant each time. Never repeat the cold-open hinge here.

---

## Beat 3 — History pivot (1:00–3:00)

Three quick historical parallels. Same mechanism, different decade. Each parallel: ~30 seconds. Format:

1. "In [year], [event happened]. Here's what people didn't see at the time…"
2. "[Year], different country. Same trick."
3. "[Year]. By now you might be noticing a pattern."

The third parallel earns the transition to the mechanism beat.

---

## Beat 4 — The mechanism (3:00–9:00)

The heart of the episode. The evergreen lesson, taught step by step. This beat is what the viewer will tell a friend about. Treat it like teaching, not arguing. Five sub-steps typical:

1. Set up the system as it normally works.
2. Identify the actor who benefits.
3. Show the lever they pull.
4. Show how the benefit compounds.
5. Show who pays the cost.

End with a single sentence naming the mechanism in plain English.

---

## Beat 5 — Back to the headline (9:00–11:00)

Apply the mechanism to today's event. Name the actual winners — specific firms, specific people, specific dollar amounts. The final 30 seconds deliver the campfire premise from the concept doc as the closing line.

No "thanks for watching." No "subscribe." The takeaway is the outro.
