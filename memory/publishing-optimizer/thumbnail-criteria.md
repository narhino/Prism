# Thumbnail criteria

Distilled from outlier thumbnail patterns in the finance-explainer niche and from general YouTube CTR research. The Publishing Optimizer reads this before reviewing a thumbnail.

## prizm-specific conventions (also see `memory/concept-developer/thumbnail-conventions.md`)
- Hand-drawn on a real whiteboard. Thick black marker outlines, bright color fill.
- News in the image, thesis in the text overlay.
- Color logic: **BLACK** = structure/facts. **RED** = the news / what's blocked. **GREEN** = money / what survives / what wins.
- 2–4 word text overlay max. Title (under the thumbnail) carries the longer thesis.

## 5-axis scoring rubric (0–10 each, total /50)

1. **Readability at 280px (mobile thumbnail size)** — Can a viewer parse the central element and text overlay in under one second on a phone? Reject if any element requires squinting.
2. **Curiosity gap** — Does the text overlay raise a question the viewer needs the video to answer? *"DOLLAR WILL SURVIVE"* over a closed strait does (why? how?). *"PETRODOLLAR EXPLAINED"* does not.
3. **News peg or recognizable anchor** — Is there a current-event element (location, logo, recognizable object) that signals what week this is? For evergreen episodes without a current event, an iconic mechanism object (vault, scales, etc.) substitutes.
4. **Visual contrast and focal hierarchy** — Bold colors with clear focal order. Eye should land on the text overlay first, then the central image, then peripheral detail. Failures: text camouflaged into the image, three elements fighting for attention.
5. **On-brand style** — Hand-drawn marker style consistent across episodes. New thumbnails should feel like they're from the same series.

A thumbnail scoring **under 7/10 on any single axis** gets specific improvement suggestions for that axis. Total score is informational, not a gate.

## Best-in-class outlier thumbnails to compare against
- **Abundantia "BlackRock owns the world"** — central logo, bold name overlay, near-white background, simple
- **Casual Finance "Brace For Impact"** — single image (cracked road) + 2-word overlay
- **Crayon Capital child-drawn style** — single character central, hand-drawn feel reinforces brand
- **The Wealth Explainer noir composition** — single character POV, dramatic lighting, minimal text

## Common failure modes
- Too many elements competing for attention (more than 1 central + 1 overlay + 1 corner detail)
- Text overlay > 4 words
- Visual that doesn't peg to news OR to an evergreen mechanism object
- No focal hierarchy — eye doesn't know where to land first
- Colors fight each other instead of carrying the channel's color logic (black/red/green)
- A face that's not the subject — distracts without paying off
- Stock-image energy — the channel signature is hand-drawn imperfection
