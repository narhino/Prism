# Title criteria

Distilled from outlier analysis of the finance-explainer YouTube niche (Crayon Capital, Billionaire Bears, The Wealth Explainer, Abundantia, Casual Finance) and from general YouTube growth research. The Publishing Optimizer reads this before authoring or critiquing a title.

## Hard rules
- Lowercase sentence case. Not Title Case.
- 6–10 words. 6–8 is the sweet spot.
- Exactly one named entity (person, company, year, place).
- A curiosity gap OR a contradiction stack — never both. Pick one.
- No clickbait the script doesn't honor (Cardinal rule from scriptwriter memory).

## Patterns that work in this niche

### Contradiction stack
> *Saudi Arabia sells dollars, not oil.*
> *Layoffs aren't a cost cut — they're a buy signal.*

Two or three short facts that conflict with each other. Crayon Capital signature ("He lied, made millions, served 22 months"). Carries the thesis upfront — evergreen-friendly.

### Curiosity gap
> *Who really controls the world's oil?*
> *Why every billionaire owns a building.*

Promises an answer the viewer has to click for. Abundantia template ("BlackRock owns the world").

### Year/date anchor
> *The 1907 Panic nobody remembers.*
> *2026 is the year the dollar refused to die.*

Casual Finance signature. Works when the year itself is the hook.

### Negative emotional word + name
> *The Saudi Aramco LIE.*
> *How CVS quietly killed drug pricing.*

Use sparingly — at most one per month. The word ALL-CAPS adds impact but burns fast.

## Patterns to reject
- Listicles (*"5 things…"*, *"7 reasons…"*) — banned per `idea-filter/rejected-patterns.md`
- Question titles that are also the answer (*"Is the dollar dying? YES."*)
- Predictions without an event (*"Stock market will crash in 2027"*)
- Title Case
- Two named entities competing for attention
- Channel name in the title (*"prizm explains the petrodollar"*) — the channel handle already does that work

## SEO trade-off rule

prizm prioritizes evergreen titles. The thumbnail carries the news peg (the news-in-image / thesis-in-text convention from `concept-developer/thumbnail-conventions.md`). Don't optimize for search keywords; optimize for browse + curiosity.

This means we accept slightly lower discoverability through search in exchange for shelf life. A petrodollar episode titled *"Saudi Arabia sells dollars, not oil."* will still get watched in 2027. A title with "Hormuz" in it expires.

## Scoring rubric (0–10)

1. **Word count in target range (6–10).** Pass/fail.
2. **Lowercase sentence case.** Pass/fail.
3. **Exactly one named entity.** Pass/fail.
4. **Pattern fit.** Does it clearly belong to one of the four working patterns above? 0–3.
5. **Evergreen survivability.** If the news cycle moves on, does the title still work? 0–3.
6. **Curiosity earned.** Does the title raise a question the viewer can't answer without watching? 0–4.

Total /10 (with rules 1-3 being mandatory pass). Titles scoring ≥8/10 ship. 5–7 get one round of revision. <5 get rebuilt.
