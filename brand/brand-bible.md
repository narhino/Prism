# Brand bible

The single source of truth for who this channel is. Every subagent reads this before doing creative work.

## Channel

- **Name:** prizm
- **One-liner:** Every headline is a transaction. We show you who's on the other side.

## Premise

When news breaks, somebody made money. We follow the receipts and explain the mechanism — a mechanism that has worked the same way for 50+ years. The headline is the doorway. The lesson is the room.

## Tone

Calm, dry, slightly skeptical investigator. Never ranty. Never hype. Never "folks" or "guys". Reads like a journalist's voiceover, not a YouTuber's. The narrator is patient, knows the audience is smart, and has already done the homework.

## Voice rules

- Short sentences default. Long ones earn their place.
- Show the mechanism, don't moralize.
- Name names — specific companies, specific people, specific dollar amounts.
- One central villain or beneficiary per episode.
- End on a one-line takeaway the viewer will repeat to a friend.

## Visual identity

- White line work on a dark charcoal background.
- Hand-drawn feel — slightly imperfect line, not vector clean.
- "Investigator's whiteboard" energy.
- Abstract human figures — no faces, no specific identifying features. Episodes need to age slowly. Same visual language for 1907 bankers and 2026 CEOs.
- Money shown as flowing lines, weighted stacks, or labeled rectangles — never photorealistic bills.

## Length

- 9–13 minutes target.
- Hard floor: 8 minutes.
- Hard ceiling: 15 minutes.

## What we avoid (lawsuit risk only)

- Naming a specific living **private** individual as the villain when the claim isn't already public record. Public figures, public officials, executives acting in their official capacity, and corporations are fair game.

Everything else — partisan framing, market calls, hot takes, financial-advice-adjacent claims, sharp opinions — is on the table. We play the grey area on purpose. The brand is the investigator who actually says what the evidence shows.
